## SOURCE 2: The Roman Empire & The 3rd Century Crisis (Currency Dilution)
- **The Problem:** During the 3rd Century AD, the Roman Empire faced massive border wars, infrastructure costs, and a bloated military payroll. The empire was physically running out of silver to pay the Roman Legions.
- **The Antoninianus (The Coin):** Introduced in 215 AD by Emperor Caracalla, the Antoninianus was initially a silver coin. 
- **The Dilution Mechanic:** Successive emperors, notably reaching a crisis point under Gallienus and Diocletian, could not balance the budget. Without the ability to print digital money, they secretly ordered the imperial mints to mix cheap base metals (copper and bronze) into the silver coins. 
- **The Math:** By the late 3rd Century, the silver content of the Antoninianus had collapsed from around 50% to less than 5%. The coin was essentially a bronze coin with a thin silver wash. 
- **The Result:** This caused explosive hyperinflation across the empire. The state "paid" its debts, but the citizens bore the cost through the destroyed purchasing power of their money.

## SOURCE 3: The Weimar Republic (1923 Hyperinflation)
- **The Problem:** Following World War I, Germany (the Weimar Republic) was saddled with massive war reparations demanded by the Treaty of Versailles, payable in gold or foreign currency, not the domestic German Papiermark.
- **The Mechanic:** Lacking the gold to pay the debt, the German government simply turned on the printing presses, printing billions of unbacked Papiermarks to buy foreign currency. 
- **The Result:** By November 1923, hyperinflation had destroyed the currency. A single loaf of bread, which cost 250 marks in January 1923, cost 200,000,000,000 (200 billion) marks by November. The state debt was technically erased, but the middle class was financially wiped out.

## SOURCE 4: Modern Mechanics (Quantitative Easing & Fractional Reserve)
- **Quantitative Easing (QE):** When the US Treasury needs money it does not have in tax revenue, it issues Treasury Bonds (debt). When there are not enough natural buyers, the Federal Reserve steps in. The Fed uses computers to create digital reserves "out of thin air" to purchase these Treasury Bonds, artificially suppressing interest rates and funding the government deficit.
- **Fractional Reserve Banking:** Commercial banks do not hold 100% of deposited cash in vaults. When a bank issues a mortgage, it does not transfer existing money; it creates new credit (money) by simply typing numbers into the borrower's account, expanding the broad money supply (M2).
- **The Conclusion (The Hidden Tax):** Modern governments do not default on debt. They systematically devalue the currency required to pay the debt. Inflation acts as a "hidden tax" on cash savers, transferring purchasing power from the citizens to the indebted state.
