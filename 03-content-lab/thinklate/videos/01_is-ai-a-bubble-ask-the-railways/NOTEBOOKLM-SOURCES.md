### Source 1: Prof. Andrew Odlyzko (2010) — The 1840s British Railway Mania
* **Document Title:** *Collective Hallucinations and Inefficient Markets: The British Railway Mania of the 1840s*
* **Author / Institution:** Prof. Andrew Odlyzko, School of Mathematics & Digital Technology Center, University of Minnesota
* **Web Link (for NotebookLM URL import):** `https://ssrn.com/abstract=1537338`
* **Direct PDF Download (for NotebookLM file upload):** `https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/hallucinations.pdf` *(or upload local file `videos/01_is-ai-a-bubble-ask-the-railways/assets/hallucinations_paper.pdf`)*
* **Key Evidence in this source:**
  * Page 1 Abstract: *"by many measures the greatest technology mania in history, and its collapse was one of the greatest financial crashes."*
  * Market Inefficiency: *"trustworthy quantitative measures existed to show investors that demand would not match revenue expectations."*
  * Famous Speculators: Charles Darwin, John Stuart Mill, and the Brontë sisters.
  * The 1830s Precursor: The 1830s mania *"collapsed prematurely"* because projects started during its exuberant phase became successful in the long run.

### Source 2: Prof. Andrew Odlyzko (2024) — The 1860s Railway Mania & Capital Scale
* **Document Title:** *The railway mania of the 1860s and financial innovation* (Revised Version, April 16, 2024)
* **Author / Institution:** Prof. Andrew Odlyzko, University of Minnesota
* **Direct PDF Download (for NotebookLM file upload):** `https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/mania18.pdf` *(or upload local file `videos/01_is-ai-a-bubble-ask-the-railways/assets/mania18_paper.pdf`)*
* **Key Evidence in this source:**
  * Page 1 Introduction: *"It turned out to be an investment disaster, but provided the country with a nationwide communication network of great utility."*
  * Page 2 & Figure 1: London Stock Exchange share price collapse (1860–1870) and Overend, Gurney crash.
  * Exact Capital Scale: *"The two railway manias of the 1840s and 1860s involved capital investments of 15 to 20% of GDP, comparable to £300 to 400 billion for UK or $3 to 4 trillion for USA today. (These were not stock market valuations, but actual funds provided by investors.)"*
