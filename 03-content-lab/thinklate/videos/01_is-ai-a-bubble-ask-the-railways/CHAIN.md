## The angle — and why it isn't "AI is a bubble"

The obvious video asserts AI is or isn't a bubble. That fails three house rules at once: it's a prediction (rule 7), it's an instance rather than an invariant (rule 4), and it's the video those two competitors already made.

**The invariant is this: a real asset and a bubble are not opposites.** Railway mania built railways that are still in use. The track survived; the shareholders did not. So the useful question is never "is it a bubble" but **"if it unwinds, who absorbs it?"** — and for a salaried engineer in India the answer runs through employment, not a portfolio.

**The analogy also breaks, and saying exactly where it breaks is the product** (see LINK 4). A channel that admits the break is doing something the AI content farms structurally cannot.

### Where this chain could be wrong — say this on screen

**LINK 4 is the load-bearing claim and the one most likely to fail.** If a material share of AI capex turns out to be financed through vendor financing, circular supplier-customer deals, or off-balance-sheet vehicles with outside equity, then the retail-wipeout channel partly *does* exist, LINK 4 weakens, and the railway parallel gets closer rather than further away. **That is a live question, not a settled one.** Name it in the video; don't bury it.

Second failure mode: LINK 3 leans on pattern-matching across episodes that differ in financing structure, regulation and scale. Three cases is not a law.
