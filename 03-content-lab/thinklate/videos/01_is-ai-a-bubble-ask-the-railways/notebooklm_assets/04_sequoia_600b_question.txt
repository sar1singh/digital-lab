TITLE: AI's $600B Question
AUTHOR: David Cahn, Partner at Sequoia Capital
PUBLICATION: Sequoia Capital Research (June 2024)

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & THE $600B REVENUE GAP:

1. THE GENESIS OF THE QUESTION
In September 2023, David Cahn published "AI's $200B Question", highlighting a massive gap between the revenue required to justify Nvidia GPU hardware investments and actual software revenue generated by AI applications. By June 2024, surging capex and revised Nvidia revenue estimates expanded this gap from $200 billion to $600 billion annually.

2. THE MATHEMATICAL FORMULATION
- Nvidia Data Center Run-Rate: Nvidia is on track to reach approximately $150 billion in annualized data center revenue.
- The 2x Hardware Multiplier: For every $1 spent on Nvidia GPUs, data center operators must spend another $1 on energy, cooling systems, specialized buildings, networking equipment, and CPU hosts (Total Cost of Ownership = $300 billion).
- The 50% Software Gross Margin Requirement: In order for the end software buyers or cloud providers to achieve a healthy 50% gross margin on their AI services, total end-user software revenues must be double the total hardware cost: $300 billion * 2 = $600 billion.
- Actual AI Revenue Today: Even with OpenAI reaching several billion dollars in annualized revenue and cloud hyperscalers monetizing AI APIs, the entire generative AI ecosystem generates an estimated $30 to $50 billion in genuine annualized software revenue.
- The Hole: $600 billion expected vs ~$50 billion actual leaves a staggering $550+ billion annual revenue hole.

3. KEY DYNAMICS & SPECULATIVE CYCLES
- GPU Supply Scarcity Has Ended: The initial GPU shortage has normalized into an oversupply / capacity glut. Startups and enterprises can now easily access compute, yet consumer and enterprise willingness to pay has not scaled proportionally.
- The "Shovel Seller" vs "Gold Miner" Dynamic: Nvidia is capturing the vast majority of cash flow in this cycle (selling the shovels), while the applications and cloud miners are experiencing severe margin compression and unproven ROI.
- Historical Parallel: Similar to the telecom bubble of the late 1990s where billions were spent laying optical fiber networks across continents, capital will be destroyed in the near-term shakeout. However, long-term winners will eventually build on top of this depreciated, low-cost compute infrastructure once financial restructuring occurs.

4. CONCLUSION & STRATEGIC LESSON
- Investors and founders must focus on genuine customer value and defensibility rather than infrastructure hoarding.
- The technology is profoundly real and transformative, but the financial timeline expected by public markets and venture capital is heavily distorted by speculative frenzy.
