## Spoken Script

In the year two thousand, neuroscientist Karim Nader proved something that terrified cognitive psychologists: the simple act of remembering your past physically melts the memory you are trying to recall! Whenever you retrieve an old memory, your brain does not perform a safe, read-only playback. Remembering unlocks and destabilizes the protein synthesis bonds holding those neurons together. For three to five hours after you think about an event, that memory turns into malleable liquid clay. If you are anxious, nostalgic, or bragging to friends over drinks, your current mood gets permanently baked into the trace. When you fall asleep that night, your brain synthesizes fresh proteins and saves the edited version right over the original file. You never remember your actual childhood. You only remember the last time you remembered it, adding five percent distortion every single round. By age thirty-five, your favorite childhood memory is a twenty-fifth-generation photocopy of a photocopy: an epic Hollywood screenplay that your own neurons wrote behind your back.
