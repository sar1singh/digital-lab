## 🎬 Beat-by-Beat Detailed Story Blueprint

### Beat 1: The Frozen Dashboard & The $18 Seat (00:00 – ~01:15)
* **Narrative Question:** *Why did a sixty-thousand-dollar car ask for eighteen dollars to warm a seat?*
* **Core Scene:** Freezing dawn morning. Driver presses heated seat button. Touchscreen displays: *"HEATED SEAT INACTIVE. SUBSCRIBE FOR £15/MONTH"*.
* **Story Circle Step 1 (You / Comfort Zone):** The illusion of consumer ownership.
* **Tension Point:** The physical copper wire and heating element are already inside the leather cushion. You paid for the car, but BMW held the warmth hostage behind cloud telemetry.

### Beat 2: The Overnight Ink Assassination (01:15 – ~02:30)
* **Narrative Question:** *Who decided your printer should die overnight?*
* **Core Scene:** Dim home office at night. Child's homework half-printed. Machine stops with a red warning dialog.
* **Story Circle Step 2 (Need / Problem):** The manufactured breakdown.
* **Evidence Spike:** HP pushed an over-the-air firmware update called "Dynamic Security" to block third-party cartridges. HP CEO Enrique Lores goes on CNBC and admits: *"Every time a customer buys a printer, it is an investment for us... if this customer doesn't print enough or doesn't use our supplies, it is a bad investment."*
* **Punchline:** You didn't buy a printer. You bought a security guard that audits your desk.

### Beat 3: The Nebraska Harvest Hostage (02:30 – ~03:45)
* **Narrative Question:** *What happens when the locked machine is a tractor?*
* **Core Scene:** Nebraska cornfield at twilight. A combine harvester sits silent.
* **Story Circle Step 3 (Go / Unfamiliar Territory):** The crisis escalates from inconvenience to livelihood survival.
* **Factual Case:** Sworn testimony of Nebraska farmer Danny Kluthe. A mechanical sensor fails; the farmer can replace the bolt in 15 minutes, but the tractor will not start until an authorized technician drives out ($230 trip charge + $130/hr labor) with proprietary Service ADVISOR software.
* **Underground Twist:** American heartland farmers buying modified firmware keygens from Eastern European forums just to harvest their own grain. The pirate respects the farmer's property rights more than the corporate manufacturer.

### Beat 4: The Wall Street Multiple Engine (03:45 – ~05:00)
* **Narrative Question:** *Why would any CEO want this?*
* **Core Scene:** Stock ticker clatter, boardroom data visualization.
* **Story Circle Step 4 (Search / Adaptation):** Unmasking the institutional machine.
* **The Math:** If a company sells hardware once, Wall Street treats it as cyclical and awards it a **1.5x revenue multiple**. But if that exact same hardware is wrapped in an annual recurring subscription, Wall Street re-labels it as "SaaS" and awards it a **6.7x ARR multiple** (median public SaaS multiple, SaaS Capital Index). A company with $1B in revenue jumps from a $1.5B valuation to $6.7B without building a single new factory.

### Beat 5: The Century-Old Precedent (Phoebus 1924) (05:00 – ~06:15)
* **Narrative Question:** *How old is this trick?*
* **Core Scene:** Geneva hotel room, Christmas Eve 1924.
* **Story Circle Step 5 (Find / Revelation):** Tracing the historical origin.
* **Historical Fact:** General Electric, Osram, and Philips created the Phoebus Cartel. Standard lightbulbs lasted 2,500 hours; the cartel secretly penalized engineers to engineer filaments that burned out at 1,000 hours.
* **Modern Evolution:** A century ago, planned obsolescence required chemists and fragile tungsten filaments. In 2026, planned obsolescence is an invisible software kill-switch pushed to your garage while you sleep.

### Beat 6: The Global Corporate Squeeze (06:15 – ~07:15)
* **Narrative Question:** *Where else did this spread?*
* **Core Scene:** Rapid evidence montage across software, automotive, and tech hardware.
* **Story Circle Step 6 (Pay / Price Paid):** The systemic reach across global markets.
* **Primary Source Grounding:**
  * **Adobe:** US FTC federal lawsuit alleging Adobe concealed 50% early termination fees behind ambushes in multi-click cancellation mazes.
  * **Indian Electric Scooters:** Ather Energy software subscription packs ($30–$40/year for dashboard features); Ola Electric software-locking hill-hold assist on base scooters.
  * **Apple:** €180 million Euroconsumers settlement across Belgium, Spain, Italy, and Portugal over undisclosed battery throttling updates that pushed consumers to upgrade.

### Beat 7: The Sovereign Escape (07:15 – ~08:00)
* **Narrative Question:** *How do we take back the keys?*
* **Core Scene:** Freezing car cockpit bookend, cut to local-first workshop and open hardware tools.
* **Story Circle Step 7 & 8 (Return & Change):** Sovereignty restored without self-help clichés.
* **Resolution:** The legal tide is turning (Colorado HB23-1011 signed in 2023 as the nation's first agricultural right-to-repair law). Consumer pushback forced BMW to abandon the heated seat subscription.
* **Closing Thesis:** Every time you choose offline storage, open hardware, and repairable tools, you send one clear message to Wall Street: We bought the machine. The keys belong to us.
