## 1. Core Paradox & Narrative Thesis
Consumers still believe that clicking "Buy" transfers legal property ownership. In reality, the entire digital economy has been converted into a **rentier licensing regime** where you own zero bytes of the software, games, media, or car features you paid for. 

Through EULAs, mandatory cloud telemetry, and firmware kill-switches, corporations retain perpetual control, extracting recurring subscription fees or deleting content from your devices without legal liability.

## 2. Hard Empirical Metrics & Legal Benchmarks

| Case / Statute | Verified Fact | Primary Source |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **California AB 2426 (Signed Sep 2024)** | Makes it illegal for digital storefronts to use the words "buy", "purchase", or "own" without explicit disclosure that consumers are only receiving a revocable license. | California Legislative Information (Statutes of 2024) |
| **Valve Steam Subscriber Agreement Update (Oct 2024)** | Steam added mandatory checkout disclaimer: *"A purchase of a digital product grants a license for the product on Steam."* | Valve Corporation Terms of Service |
| **Sony PlayStation Discovery Removal (Dec 2023)** | Sony notified users it was wiping previously purchased Warner Bros / Discovery TV shows from user libraries due to licensing expiration. | Official PlayStation Support Notice |
| **Ubisoft 'The Crew' Shutdown (April 2024)** | Ubisoft shut down authentication servers for a 2014 game and revoked the game license from players' Ubisoft Connect libraries, sparking a class-action lawsuit. | U.S. District Court, Northern District of California |
| **BMW Heated Seat Micro-Subscription ($18/month)** | Attempt to charge $18/month or $415 unlimited to activate pre-installed hardware heated seats via OTA software. Abandoned in 2023 after intense backlash. | BMW Official Board Press Statement |
| **Stop Killing Games Initiative (2024–2026)** | Global consumer movement demanding that publishers leave games in a playable state when sunsetting online servers. | European Citizens' Initiative (ECI) |

## 3. Recurring Economic Patterns & Analogies
* **The Ghost Landlord:** You bought the living room sofa, but the manufacturer has a remote control that locks the cushions unless you pay a monthly sitting fee.
* **The First Sale Doctrine Destruction:** How the 1908 Supreme Court *Bobbs-Merrill Co. v. Straus* ruling (establishing the right to resell physical books) was bypassed by defining everything as a digital license rather than a good.
