## 4. The lens

> **Who decided this, what were they optimising for, and why is it still shaped that way — and how does that reach your life?**

Core move: take well-documented history, find the pattern, apply it to something happening now, **then follow the chain down to the individual.** *"This isn't new. Here's what happened last time — and here's how it arrives in your rent, your job, your choices."*

**Macro-to-micro is the differentiator.** Wendover, B1M, Johnny Harris, Patrick Boyle and Think School all stop at the macro level. Closing the loop to the viewer's own life is the gap. It also widens the audience from "people interested in trade policy" to "people who noticed their life changed and don't know why."

**Meta-thesis (the reason to subscribe):** in an AI-saturated information environment, the scarce skill is no longer access to information — it's **observation and pattern recognition.** Every video should leave the viewer with a sharper eye, not just a fact.

**Tech is the substrate, not a topic.** Every subject here happens *because of* technology, *with* it, *for* it, or *by* it. That four-way taxonomy is also the cheapest topic generator in this folder, and technology is the **shortest chain to the micro landing** — 1–2 documented links versus 4–5 inferred ones for currency policy.

**Two content lines, not one** (`21_VISION.md` §8): **Explanation** — the systems acting on you — builds trust, and is videos 1–12 unchanged. **Demonstration** — real people who left, with real numbers — produces the courage, and gets added from ~video 10. Information does not produce action; visible proof does.

**Full treatment: `10_MACRO-TO-MICRO.md` and `11_TECH-AS-SUBSTRATE.md`.** Read both before scripting.

Captures current-affairs search demand while the content stays evergreen, because mechanism and precedent don't expire.

**Why the lens solves the hard problem:** Sarwan has no entrepreneurial track record. Hindsight analysis requires none — you examine what already happened rather than advising. It also removes the faceless credibility problem: nobody needs to trust the narrator to trust a well-sourced explanation.

## 6. Editorial rules

1. **Two-year test** — if the video stops making sense in two years, don't make it
2. **Never state a number not seen in a source actually opened.** AI may summarise; AI may not supply facts
3. Cite on screen — source name and date, every figure
4. Opinion allowed where the work was done; state uncertainty plainly otherwise
5. No regulated advice
6. No employer material
7. Pattern recognition is the product — facts may be well known, the synthesis must be original
8. Never end on a prediction. End on a better question
9. **Label every link in the causal chain** — DOCUMENTED / LIKELY / PLAUSIBLE / SPECULATIVE. Never present an inferred link with a documented link's confidence. The label is the product
10. **Anchor every chain in the financial or career domain.** Social and psychological effects may be added on top, clearly marked as inference — never load-bearing
11. **Offer a pattern to notice, never an action to take.** Explaining why you feel this is analysis; telling you what to do is advice, and advice is out of scope
12. **Cover the invariant, not the instance.** Use the current technology as the example, never the subject. If a title contains a product name or version number, rewrite it
