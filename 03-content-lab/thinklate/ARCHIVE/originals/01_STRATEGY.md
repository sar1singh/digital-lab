## 3. Editorial rules

Non-negotiable. These exist because you said opinion would be "case by case" — without rules, the voice drifts.

1. **The two-year test.** If the video stops making sense in two years, don't make it. This is the single rule that keeps you out of the commentary trap.
2. **Never state a number you haven't seen in a source you opened.** AI may summarise; AI may not supply facts. One fabricated chart ends the channel.
3. **Cite on screen.** Source name and date visible when a figure or quote appears.
4. **Opinion is allowed where you've done the work.** State uncertainty plainly otherwise. "I don't know" is on-brand for a hindsight channel.
5. **No regulated advice.** No securities, no returns, no "what to invest in", no medical, no legal. SEBI restricts unregistered finfluencers; YouTube's July 2026 policy demonetises AI-narrated finance, legal, health and medical content.
6. **No employer material.** No Cox Automotive specifics, no internal examples, no enterprise-codebase anecdotes, no automotive retail or dealership topics. Personal hardware and accounts only.
7. **Pattern recognition is the product.** Facts can be well known. The synthesis must be yours — that's both the value and the anti-slop defence.
8. **No AI-generated imagery.** AI *charts from verified data* are data visualisation. AI *images* are a slop signal. Use the first, never the second.

## 6. Production pipeline

Built around your stated bottleneck: **asset gathering killed your motivation before.** This pipeline removes stock-footage hunting entirely.

### The visual system

**Document-and-data aesthetic.** Charts you generate, maps, timelines, annual-report and filing excerpts, archival images, screen-recorded scroll-throughs of real documents. This is Patrick Boyle's and Modern MBA's entire look — minimal footage, mostly charts and text on screen.

It is faster than stock hunting **and** it reads as more researched. In 2026, stock-montage-plus-AI-voice is the slop signature; charts from real data are the opposite.

### Asset sourcing — risk tiers

| Source | Risk |
|---|---|
| Charts you build from public data | Clean |
| Government, central bank, World Bank, IMF data | Clean |
| Public-domain and archival images | Clean |
| Brief article/newspaper excerpts for commentary | Defensible but not blanket-safe. Keep short, on screen briefly, always attributed on screen |
| Paywalled article screenshots | **Avoid** |
| AI-generated images | **Avoid** — slop signal |

### Per-video workflow

| Stage | Hours | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Topic selection | 0.5 | From a standing backlog, not from scratch |
| Research | 2–3 | Feed real filings/reports/papers into NotebookLM; it summarises *your* documents with citations back to them. It is not a fact oracle |
| Outline + pattern | 1 | Your original contribution. Don't outsource this |
| Script | 1.5–2 | Write it, then read aloud and cut every clause you stumble on. Short sentences, one idea each |
| Chart & frame build | 2–3 | The replacement for stock hunting |
| Record voice | 0.5–1 | Your own voice. See §7 |
| Edit | 2–3 | |
| Package (title, thumbnail, description, subtitles) | 1 | Accurate subtitles, not auto-generated |
| **Total** | **~11–15** | ≈ one video per week at your available hours |

**Cadence: every two weeks.** Use the Floor format (6–8 min, 4–5 hrs) on weeks when the Standard format isn't possible — see `09_COUNTER-STRATEGY.md` §3.

## 7. Voice

- **Use your own voice from video 1.** Three reasons: it's an authenticity signal post-July-2026; it's free; and it preserves continuity if you show your face later. An AI voice now means breaking continuity later.
- **Changing voice mid-series measurably hurts returning-viewer rates.** Pick once, commit.
- Accent is not your constraint — your previous videos got 15–30 views, so nobody heard it. Packaging and consistency are the constraints.
- What does cost retention is articulation and pacing, and that's a scripting fix, not an accent fix.
- Accurate subtitles remove most remaining friction and help search.
- **Test, don't debate:** publish 3 videos, then read the retention curve and audience geography in Analytics. Real data in six weeks beats speculation now.

---

## 8. Packaging

- Average video loses **half its viewers in the first 90 seconds**. Hook discipline matters more than anything else in this document.
- Open with the question, not the context. Context comes after the viewer is committed.
- **Design for the TV screen** — YouTube reports over a billion hours of daily TV watch time, and long-form explainer content is what that surface rewards. Legible text, slower pacing, thumbnails that read at ten feet.
- Titles: question or claim, never a topic label. "Why Tariffs Always Disappoint" beats "Tariffs Explained."
- **Length is the metric lever.** YPP counts watch *hours*, not views. A 25-minute video at 40% retention yields 10 minutes per view and halves the views you need.

**Honest caveat:** analysis audiences convert worse than "how to make money" audiences. Alux sells aspiration; you're selling understanding. Lower conversion, higher trust, better sponsors. Know this before building the product tier.

### YPP thresholds — the 2027 change

| | Now | From 1 Feb 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Long-form | 1,000 subs + **4,000** watch hours / 365d | 1,000 subs + **8,000** watch hours / 365d |
| Shorts | 1,000 subs + **10M** views / 90d | 1,000 subs + **20M** views / 90d |

- Creators already in YPP are unaffected. Fan Funding and Shopping thresholds unchanged.
- **The Shorts path is closed to you** — 20M engaged views in 90 days is incompatible with 15 hrs/week. Long-form only.
- Qualified watch hours require **public long-form or archived livestreams**. Private, unlisted, deleted, watched-as-ad, and un-archived streams don't count.
- **Beating 1 Feb 2027 is a stretch bonus, not the plan.** Realistically 10–20% for a channel starting from zero. Build for the 8,000-hour bar; take 4,000 if early videos overperform.

## 10. Metrics and kill criteria

Review at video 12 and video 24.

| Metric | Healthy at video 12 |
|---|---|
| Average view duration | > 35% |
| 30-second retention | > 70% |
| Subscribers | > 200 |
| Impressions CTR | > 4% |
| Newsletter signups | > 50 |

**Kill / pivot triggers**

- 12 videos published and median under 200 views → packaging problem. Fix titles and thumbnails before changing topic.
- 30-second retention under 50% → hook or voice problem. Diagnose in that order.
- You miss publishing four weeks in a row → the pipeline is too heavy. Cut video length, not frequency.

Diagnostic from prior attempt: @sarwanology published 9 videos across 3 years with no consistent topic. That, not content quality, is why it sits at 21 subscribers. Consistency is the variable being tested here.
