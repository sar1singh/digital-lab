## PART 3 — Voice and scripting guide

**Your stated voice:** friendly, direct, sarcastic or satirical, hard facts. Not robotic, not formal, loose and conversational.

That's a real register and it has a proven model on YouTube: **Patrick Boyle** — deadpan, dry, data-led, quietly savage, never shouty. Study his pacing. The comedy is in the understatement and in what he *declines* to say.

### The one rule that makes sarcasm work

> **Aim the sarcasm at decisions and systems. Never at people, and never at viewers.**

- **Ages well:** "The plan was to reroute global shipping around an entire continent. This was considered the good option."
- **Ages badly:** "[Named official] is an idiot." — dates instantly, invites pile-ons, costs you sponsors, and conflicts with staying low-profile while employed.

Sarcasm without rigour reads as smugness. The facts must carry the joke — the joke is the delivery, never the substance.

### Writing for speech, not for reading

> **One correction from `12_CRAFT-EVIDENCE.md`:** the "short sentences" rule below produces *uniform* pacing, and sentence-length **variance** retains ~1.8× better. Vary deliberately — long, then short. Keep everything else.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Contractions — "it's", "doesn't", "wasn't" | "it is", "does not" |
| Sentence fragments. On purpose. | Full formal sentences throughout |
| Second person — "you", "your" | "one might consider" |
| One idea per sentence | Subordinate clauses stacked three deep |
| Short paragraphs — 2–3 sentences | Walls of text |
| Say the number, then what it means | Recite numbers without interpretation |
| Asides in a lower register | Formal parentheticals |

**The read-aloud test is non-negotiable.** Write it, read it out loud, and cut every clause you stumble on. If you can't say it naturally, rewrite it. This does more for your delivery than any voice coaching.

### Before / after

**Robotic (don't):**
> The Suez Canal represents a critical maritime chokepoint through which approximately twelve percent of global trade transits annually. Recent disruptions have necessitated the rerouting of vessels via the Cape of Good Hope, resulting in significant increases in transit times and associated costs.

**Your voice (do):**
> Roughly twelve percent of world trade goes through a ditch in Egypt.
>
> A ditch. One hundred and ninety-three kilometres of it, dug by hand in the 1860s, and we have built the entire global economy on the assumption that it stays open.
>
> It didn't. So ships started going around Africa instead — which adds about ten days and a great deal of money, and was, remarkably, the *sensible* option.

Same facts. One sounds like a consultancy report; the other sounds like a person who finds this genuinely absurd.

### Script structure — SUPERSEDED

> **Use the boomerang structure in `10_MACRO-TO-MICRO.md` §3 instead.** The version below predates the macro-to-micro positioning and is kept only for the section timings.

#### Original structure (18–30 min)

| Section | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 0:00–0:30 | The question, sharply. No intro, no logo, no "hey guys." Half your viewers leave in 90 seconds — spend none of it on housekeeping |
| **Stakes** | 0:30–1:30 | Why this matters to the viewer *now*. One live fact |
| **The history** | 1:30–10:00 | The documented case. Charts and sources on screen. Your strongest material |
| **The pattern** | 10:00–16:00 | **Your original contribution.** What repeats, and why. This is the product |
| **Now** | 16:00–22:00 | Apply the pattern to the live situation. State uncertainty plainly |
| **Close** | last 60s | One line that reframes the opening question. Newsletter CTA. Stop |

**Never end on a prediction.** End on a better question. That's the brand — hindsight, not forecasting — and it's also the honest position.

### Signature devices (use sparingly — 2–3 per video)

- **The deadpan restatement:** state the absurd thing plainly, add nothing. The fact does the work.
- **The number that lands:** "Ninety percent. Of the advanced chips. On one island."
- **The unearned confidence callback:** quote what people said at the time, then show what happened.
- **The admitted limit:** "I don't know how this ends. Here's what I do know." Builds more trust than any confident take.

### Hard limits

- No predictions stated as certainty
- No "they don't want you to know this" framing — conspiracy register destroys sponsor viability
- No mockery of individuals, especially named living people
- No regulated advice — no securities, returns, "what to buy", medical or legal
- Never a number you haven't seen in a source you opened
- Cite on screen: source name and date, every figure

### Robotic-delivery checklist

Before recording, check the script for:

- [ ] Any sentence over 25 words → split it
- [ ] "Furthermore", "moreover", "in conclusion", "it is important to note" → delete
- [ ] Three or more numbers in one sentence → break apart
- [ ] Any paragraph you can't read in one breath → rewrite
- [ ] Passive voice where active works → fix
- [ ] Does it sound like *you* talking to one person? If it sounds like a presentation, rewrite
